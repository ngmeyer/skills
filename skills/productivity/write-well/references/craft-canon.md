# The craft canon — deduped positive ruleset

The load-bearing prose rules from the major style authorities, deduped (shared rules credited to the originator + key reinforcers, not stacked). The five-part core in SKILL.md is the spine; this is the complete set. Grouped by what each rule does.

## Clarity & concision
1. **Omit needless words** — every word must do work. *(Strunk; Orwell "if it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out"; Zinsser; even Pinker calls this "the most important maxim of good prose")*
2. **Characters as subjects, actions as verbs** — readers feel prose is clear when the subjects name the cast and the verbs name what they do. *(Williams — the single strongest positive clarity engine)*
3. **Kill nominalizations** — turn the abstract noun back into the verb it came from (*decision → decide*); nominalizations are "the primary cause of unclear, abstract prose." *(Williams; Lanham)*
4. **Active by default; passive on purpose** — prefer active, but use the passive when the acted-upon is the reader's current focus. *(Strunk/Orwell for the default; Pinker for the deliberate exception — the blanket ban is folklore)*
5. **Get to the main verb fast** — avoid long wind-ups and long abstract subjects. *(Williams; Lanham "start fast")*
6. **Prefer the short, everyday word** to the long or jargon word. *(Orwell; Zinsser)*
7. **Positive over negative** — say what is, not what isn't. *(Strunk; Williams)*
8. **Clear thinking is clear writing** — murky prose means murky thought; ask "What am I trying to say? Have I said it?" *(Zinsser)*
9. **Beat the curse of knowledge** — you're too close to the subject to see what the reader doesn't know; awareness alone won't fix it — use a test reader. *(Pinker)*

### The Paramedic Method (Lanham) — a mechanical concision pass for edit mode
1. Circle the prepositions. 2. Circle the "be" verbs. 3. Ask "Where's the action? Who's doing it?" 4. Put the doer in the subject, the action in a simple active verb. 5. Start fast — cut the wind-up. 6. Read aloud. (Optional: compute the "lard factor" = words removed ÷ original; 50% is typical in bloated prose.)

## Rhythm & sound
10. **Vary sentence length to make music** — strings of equal-length sentences drone. *(Provost; Le Guin; Lanham)*
11. **Read it aloud** — the ear catches what the eye skims. *(Lanham; Le Guin "The Sound of Your Writing")*
12. **Right-branch** — subject + verb early, modifiers trailing, so even a long sentence stays clear. *(Clark)*
13. **Emphatic word last** — put the strong/new/heavy material at the end of the sentence. *(Strunk; Williams; Clark)*
14. **Cohesion: open old, close new** — begin a sentence with familiar information, end with the new. This is what makes paragraphs *flow* between sentences, not just shine individually. *(Williams — the highest-value rule the anti-AI references lacked)*

## Concreteness & word choice
15. **Definite, specific, concrete** — write with nouns and verbs. *(Strunk; Pinker endorses)*
16. **No stale figures of speech** — avoid any metaphor/simile you're used to seeing in print; invent a fresh one or use none. *(Orwell)*
17. **Let the meaning choose the word**, not the word the meaning. *(Orwell)*

## Structure
18. **Start somewhere, go somewhere, sit down** — beginning, middle, end. *(McPhee)*
19. **Structure from the material, kept invisible** — let it arise from the content, "about as visible as someone's bones." *(McPhee)*
20. **Write the lead first** — it unlocks the structure of everything after. *(McPhee)*
21. **Chronology vs theme** — expect the tension; chronology usually wins, but choose consciously. *(McPhee)*
22. **Crowd and leap** (Le Guin) — pack the sentence with what matters and is interconnected ("load every rift with ore"); leap over the rest, trusting white space.
23. **Gold coins + a central question** — reward the reader with a nugget every few hundred words; use internal cliffhangers; build the piece around one question. *(Clark)*
24. **Write in scenes, tell stories** — narrative carries information faster than exposition; even abstract prose has characters and actions underneath. *(Williams; Clark; Elmore Leonard via Clark — "leave out the part readers skip")*

## Voice & stance
25. **Humanity** — clarity, simplicity, brevity *and* warmth; "an aliveness that keeps the reader reading." Clarity alone isn't enough. *(Zinsser)*
26. **Classic style** — prose is a window onto the world; show the reader what you see, as an equal in conversation. The default stance for essay / newsletter / blog. *(Pinker, after Thomas & Turner)*

## The meta-rule
27. **Rules are defaults with reasons, not commandments.** Keep the positive maxims; understand *why* a rule exists, then break it when it serves the reader (Orwell's own rule vi: "Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous"). The grammar *superstitions* — never the passive, never split an infinitive, never open with "however," never end on a preposition — are folklore that often make prose worse (Pinker; Pullum). **Inviolable:** voice, specificity, clear thinking. **Calibration-dependent:** em-dash budget, tricolon limit, passive voice, sentence-fragment use.

> **Do NOT import** the grammar prohibitions as hard rules. Strunk and Orwell both used the passive in the very sentences banning it. Adding those superstitions would make write-well the rigid rule-bot its own thesis warns against.

## Genre re-weighting
Same rules, different ranking by surface:
- **Fiction** — rhythm/sound, scene + crowding-leaping, concrete sensory image, voice; structure invisible. Concision is subordinate to music; fragments/passive are fair game for effect.
- **Essay / long-form** — classic style, story-first lead → principle, cohesion, central question + gold coins, warmth.
- **Marketing / copy** — concrete specific outcome over vague benefit, emphatic word last, one central promise, tiny-story testimonials; kill hedging and promotional abstraction.
- **Technical / reference** — Williams' clarity (characters/actions, fast verb), Lanham's lard-cutting, curse-of-knowledge discipline. **Classic style does NOT apply** — a manual needs instructions, not a dialogue; the passive is fine when the system/result is the focus.
- **Newsletter / blog** — classic style: conversational equal-to-equal voice, gold coins, internal cliffhangers, varied rhythm; looser structure than the essay, but the lead still does the work.
